The proof procurement asks for.

When a buyer asks for your VPAT, they're asking a fair question: can everyone use what you're selling? A VPAT (the Voluntary Product Accessibility Template) is the standard way to answer, and once it's filled out it earns a second name: an Accessibility Conformance Report, or ACR. We prepare yours from real testing, and we walk you through every line of it.

First, the two acronyms

VPAT
The blank form. The Voluntary Product Accessibility Template is published by the Information Technology Industry Council (ITI), the trade association much of the tech industry belongs to. It lists every accessibility requirement a buyer might care about and asks the same question of each: does your product meet it?
ACR
The form, filled out. With every answer in place, the template becomes an Accessibility Conformance Report: the document your buyer actually reads. Most people say “VPAT” for both, the way a filled-out application is still called an application. Use whichever name you like; we always know what you mean.

The distinction matters in exactly one place: the thing procurement needs is the completed report, with honest answers and evidence behind them. That's the one we build.

Where the answers come from

Every answer traces to a tested finding.

The template asks the same question of every requirement in scope, most of them success criteria from the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), and accepts three honest answers: Supports, Partially Supports, and Does Not Support. The middle answer carries more weight than you might expect. Procurement reads a candid Partially Supports, with a remark explaining the gap and the fix underway, as the mark of a vendor who actually tested. A page of nothing but Supports gets read twice.

That's why our VPATs come after an audit, never from a self-assessment questionnaire. Each answer in your ACR is backed by what our auditors found by hand: the finding, the evidence, the remark. If you haven't been audited yet, that's simply where we start; see how an audit runs, and the VPAT follows from it.

One template, four editions

Standards differ by market, so ITI publishes the current template, VPAT 2.5, in four editions. Choosing one is the first scoping question, and we'll tell you plainly which your buyers expect. Every price below is for the document alone, added to your audit: we can only report on what we've tested.

WCAG edition
Measures against the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines alone. The broadest edition, and the right one when buyers simply want to know the product is accessible.

508 edition

EU edition

What buyers do with it

Procurement reads these for a living.

Government and education buyers are often required to collect an ACR before they can sign, and enterprise procurement checklists ask for one by name. Having yours ready means the request stops being a scramble and becomes an attachment.

Plenty of companies go a step further and publish their ACR right on their site, beside their accessibility statement. It answers the question before anyone asks it, and it tells every visitor the product takes this seriously. When yours is ready, we'd encourage you to do the same.
