How we test

We meet your product the way your users do.

NVDA, VoiceOver, JAWS, and TalkBack are screen readers: software that reads the screen aloud, or sends it to a braille display, for people who are blind or have low vision. Every audit runs NVDA and VoiceOver by default, covering Windows, Mac, and iPhone between them. If your users rely on JAWS or on Android's TalkBack, we add those on request. Keyboard-only testing comes standard on every engagement.

The heart of every audit is a person, sitting with your product, using it. Our auditors navigate your critical journeys with the same assistive technology your users bring: listening to how a form announces itself, feeling where keyboard focus lands, noticing the exact moment a journey quietly becomes impossible.

Where automation fits

Scanners catch about a quarter of the problems.

We run automated checks on every engagement, led by axe, the engine behind much of the industry's tooling. It sweeps each page for the mechanical issues a machine can see: missing image descriptions, contrast slips, code that tells assistive technology one thing and does another. It's fast, it's tireless, and we'd be silly not to use it.

What a scanner can't tell you is whether the focus order makes sense, whether the alt text says anything useful, or whether checking out with a screen reader takes four minutes or forty. That judgment is the audit. Automation clears the mechanical layer so our auditors spend their time on the three quarters only a person can find.

How an audit runs

  1. 1

    Step 1: Scope

    Tell us about your product and the markets you sell into; if it's live, include the URL. Within one business day you'll have a fixed-fee proposal in plain language: the pages, the flows, the price.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Test

    Our auditors work through everything in scope by hand, with automated sweeps clearing the mechanical layer as they go. The default is WCAG 2.2 at Level AA, the level most laws require: all 55 of its checkpoints, called success criteria. If your regulation calls for WCAG 2.1, or you want the stricter 2.2 AAA level, we test to that instead.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Report

    Findings land in Labrador (the testing platform we built) as they're discovered, and your report is generated straight from that record: severity, evidence, and suggested fixes, nothing retyped.

  4. 4

    Step 4: Walk through

    We sit down with your team and put the findings in order together, so you leave with a clear picture and a sensible plan.

  5. 5

    Step 5: Re-test

    When your fixes have been made, we verify them and update the report. And if you'd like assistance, we can have our developers make the fixes.

What you get

Every audit closes with the same set of deliverables, built to be used rather than filed.

  • Findings you can hand to a developer

    Every issue is written against its WCAG success criterion, with severity, evidence (screenshots and video where they help), and a suggested fix.

  • A report people actually read

    An interactive HTML report, generated by Labrador, the testing platform we built. Filter by page, severity, or criterion; every claim sits next to its evidence.

  • Exports that meet your team where it works

    A Jira-ready CSV export, or a direct Jira integration, moves findings straight into your backlog, and a plain CSV opens anywhere a spreadsheet does. When procurement asks for formal proof, your audit becomes the basis for a VPAT (the industry's Voluntary Product Accessibility Template) or an ACR, an Accessibility Conformance Report, which is simply a filled out VPAT.

  • A walkthrough, included

    We present what we found, answer your questions, and help you decide what to fix first, so the report starts moving the day it lands.
