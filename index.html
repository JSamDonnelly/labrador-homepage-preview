Our philosophy

Access to the internet is a civil right.

We mean that literally. One of our founders taught UX in Haiti and co-founded a digital-literacy nonprofit there. Getting people online is one half of access. Accessibility is the other half. School, work, and public life happen online now, and a connection means little if what loads cannot be used.

That is why we don't ration attention. Every client gets as much of it as they need, for as long as they need it, and we work with them until WCAG 2.2 and regulatory conformance are met.

Our tool

We built the software our audits run on.

Labrador is our guided WCAG conformance testing platform, and it is most of why our audits come back consistent. Testers work each success criterion in order, page by page and component by component, recording severity and remediation status as they go, so nothing depends on a spreadsheet someone forgot to update.

Evidence lands in the same record. Screenshots and video attach to the finding they document, and your report is generated from that record rather than retyped into it. Every export is built for the person receiving it: beautiful, interactive HTML reports with embedded media and clickable criterion links, an editable DOCX VPAT when procurement asks for one, and a Jira-ready export plus a Jira integration that moves findings straight into your developers' backlog.

It is not a scanner, and it is not something you install on your site. It gives the manual work structure instead of trying to replace it.

On widgets and overlays

Accessibility isn't something you can install.

Widgets promise compliance in a line of JavaScript. They sit on top of a site and patch it in the browser, but the barriers live in the code underneath, and that is where they stay. Often the widget adds new ones, intercepting keyboard focus or overriding the screen reader someone has already tuned to their liking.

The filings bear it out. UsableNet, which tracks ADA website lawsuits, finds that more than one in five are now brought against sites that already had a widget running.

If you have one today, that is not a mark against you. Plenty of teams were sold one in good faith. Ask us and we will tell you plainly what it does and does not cover.
